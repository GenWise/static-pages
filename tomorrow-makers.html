Tomorrow Makers is a national programme of the Godrej Foundation that finds bright students from underserved backgrounds and gives them the sustained mentoring most of them would never otherwise reach. GenWise designs and runs its STEM track – weekly online sessions in Science, Mathematics and computational thinking, beginning in middle school. Free for every student.

GenWise is a Programme Partner of Tomorrow Makers, an initiative of the Godrej Foundation – alongside Avanti Fellows, Dakshana Foundation, MakerGhat, Slam Out Loud and others, with student assessments by Educational Initiatives, the makers of ASSET. More at tomorrow-makers.in.

Why this programme

In every school, in every district, there are children who are well ahead of their class – curious, quick, hungry for harder problems. For a decade GenWise has worked with such students through its residential summer programmes and Gifted Lab, where at least a fifth of every residential cohort comes from less privileged backgrounds on full scholarships. But a scholarship seat at a time is not a system.

Tomorrow Makers is the system: a national talent search that finds these students wherever they are, and year-round pathways – STEM, invention and making, arts, endurance and service – that grow with them, shaped by their talent and effort rather than their circumstance. GenWise brings to its STEM track the same mentors and the same way of teaching that our fee-paying programmes are built on.

How it works

Finding the students

Students in Grades 6–8 register through the Tomorrow Makers platform and take a staged assessment designed by Educational Initiatives. The search runs nationally – the point is to find the child in a small town whose ability would otherwise go unnoticed.

Weekly mentoring, online

  • A live session every week, online, in small groups of about thirty students with a dedicated coach who stays with the group.
  • Sessions designed by GenWise mentors – experienced educators and practitioners – and delivered by coaches trained and supervised by them, so the programme can reach many groups without thinning out.
  • Science, Mathematics and computational thinking, taught the GenWise way: start from a real problem, argue from evidence, let the ideas earn their names.

Free for every student

The programme is funded by the Godrej Foundation. No student pays a fee at any stage – not for the assessment, not for the sessions.

In session

Three sessions from the programme's opening cohort, for Grades 6–8.

Instructing a literal-minded computer

Students direct a "computer" that does exactly what it is told – no more, no less – to reproduce a drawing using only words. Through repeated failures they invent sequence, reference points, definitions and measurement for themselves.

Whose formula is it anyway?

Area and perimeter, misconception-first: measure everyday objects, estimate irregular shapes, and generalise your own formulas – then spend the week noticing area in the world around you.

Science with moong beans

Sprout beans under different treatments, argue about what counts as a fair comparison, and meet control groups and messy real-world data – science as testable questions, not facts to memorise.

The programme, briefly

  • A national programme of the Godrej Foundation, launched in 2026
  • GenWise designs and delivers the STEM mentoring track
  • Students in Grades 6–8 to begin with, growing with the cohorts through high school
  • Selection through a national talent search, with assessments by Educational Initiatives
  • Weekly live online sessions in groups of ~30, each with a dedicated coach
  • Sessions designed by GenWise mentors, delivered by trained coaches
  • First cohorts underway in 2026, growing to several hundred students nationwide
  • Free for every student, at every stage
