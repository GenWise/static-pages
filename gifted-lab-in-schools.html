Why this age matters

Every school has children working well beyond their grade level, but few have a sustained structure to keep them challenged. Ages 10–14 are the window to turn potential into durable ways of thinking. By Grade 9, boards and admissions leave much less room for this work.

Gifted Lab provides that structure while children remain in their own school and community. Singapore is moving the same way. After forty years of identifying gifted nine-year-olds and transferring them to a handful of special schools, its Ministry of Education is replacing that programme from 2027: children stay in their own school, and those who need more take a weekly two-hour advanced module with academic peers — explicitly not exam preparation, and with several chances to be identified rather than one. Gifted Lab applies the same principle, with the module inside your own school rather than at a centre.

Who joins

Gifted Lab is designed for children working in roughly the top 10% of their year group. Schools that already run a standardised assessment — the Ei-ASSET Talent Search from Educational Initiatives, the NWEA MAP tests, or something comparable — can identify them from those results. Where a school uses none of these, school or teacher recommendation is enough, and GenWise will assess candidates online at no cost to the school.

What the learning looks like

Where does the energy go when a diver hits the water?

Students defended the 18th-century caloric theory of heat, then hunted for the experiment that breaks it. By the end they could say not just that caloric theory is wrong, but what would have had to be true for it to be right. Science as evidence-based argument, not memorisation.

Can paper do geometry better than a ruler and compass?

Students folded, cut and repaired paper models. Only after an object worked did the mathematics behind it get named. A difficult cutting challenge became a problem about symmetry.

"I wasn't expecting it to be so mathematics related… happily surprised."

Progress without tests or grades

We use short formative checks, open investigations and children's own work. Reports focus on the quality of reasoning, explanation and persistence, not marks.

A room of academic peers

Children experience being wrong among equals, choosing the hard problem and working through frustration. That reveals strengths and needs a regular classroom may not show.

The year, briefly

  • 30 sessions per subject across the academic year, arranged in six 5-week modules
  • Science and Mathematics two to three grade levels ahead: depth first, not the syllabus early
  • One to two hours of independent work each week per subject, mostly open exploration

Questions school leaders ask

What about the classes children miss?

We schedule around the school's examinations and tests. The coordinator also watches each child's progress in regular subjects; if a child begins to slip, GenWise and the school agree on the right way forward.

Does taking children out create a divide?

Schools already recognise that different learners need different provision. Gifted Lab extends that principle to children working well ahead, while keeping them in their own school. Participants also share their learning through activities for younger classes and an annual student-led Mathematics and Science day.

Will a stronger peer group affect confidence?

Part-time provision keeps a child's place in their regular classroom while giving them one morning among academic peers. A 2022 study of 292 Grade 5–6 students compared exactly these three situations — a weekly mathematics pull-out programme, a full-time separate gifted programme, and no gifted provision at all. Children in the weekly pull-out reported more confidence in their mathematics ability than those with no provision, and less perfectionism and loneliness than those in full-time separate classes (Cash & Lin, Gifted Child Quarterly, 2022). One study is not the whole literature, but it examines the choice a school actually faces.

What if we cannot find ten children in a grade band?

Those children can join the after-school online programme, which runs on Friday evenings and at weekends. The school band can move on campus at the start of any later module once it reaches ten.

What happens if we say yes?

We begin with a thirty-minute call. If the model fits, we agree the modules, identify candidates, communicate with parents, train the coordinator and check the room. The first module can usually begin within five to six weeks — at whichever module boundary falls next, which need not be the start of the academic year.
